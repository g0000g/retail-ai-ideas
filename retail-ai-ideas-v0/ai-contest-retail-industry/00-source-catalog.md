## 1 · NetSuite — "16 AI in Retail Use Cases & Examples" ◐

<https://www.netsuite.com/portal/resource/articles/erp/retail-ai.shtml>

**Framing:** customer expectations for speed, convenience and personalization — especially online;
retailers struggle to deliver in-the-moment personalized experiences. AI positioned across the value chain:
inventory management & logistics, customer analytics & forecasting, shopper engagement & personalization.

**Stat:** 40% of retail executives say their business uses some form of intelligent automation.

**Use cases recovered:**
- Inventory management — AI predicts customer demand from historical purchase data + supply chain analytics
- Demand forecasting — historical sales + present-day market info + emerging shopping trends; forecasts adjust as conditions change
- Assortment planning
- Real-time personalization
- Visual product search
- Personalized product recommendations (from purchasing history)
- AI-powered inventory robots in warehouses — fill orders automatically, flag misplaced or low-stock items
- Perishables example — grocery store analyses historical inventory + customer-demand data to refine how it stores and displays perishables
- Automated customer support and checkout *(from the companion automation article)*
- Simplified return processing *(same)*
- Supply chain optimization *(same)*
- Shopper behaviour analysis *(same)*
- Personalized marketing campaigns *(same)*

**Challenges named (this is the most useful part of the source):**
- unify fragmented data
- protect privacy
- maintain recommendation quality
- coordinate channels
- **connect promotions with available inventory**
- **review AI decisions**
- **measure real business impact**
- inaccurate inventory, disconnected orders, inconsistent product data and fragmented customer records prevent retailers acting on insights

> These challenges are the direct origin of [I06 — AI governance & impact measurement](I06-ai-governance-measurement.md).

## 2 · InsiderOne — "AI in Retail: 10 Breakthrough Trends" ●

<https://insiderone.com/ai-retail-trends/>

**Stats:** 78% of organizations reported using AI in 2024, up from 55% (Stanford AI Index);
1,950% YoY increase in retail site traffic from chat interactions during 2024's Cyber Monday (Adobe).

1. **AI shopping assistants and virtual agents** — intent prediction from behavioural cues, real-time product guidance using site search + purchase history, omnichannel across web/app/messaging. Outcomes: lower search friction and bounce, higher AOV and CLTV.
2. **Hyper-personalization and predictive engagement** — behavioural/transactional/contextual data, dynamic recommendations, predictive segmentation, **Send-Time Optimization**, **Next-Best-Channel prediction**. Case: Slazenger — 49× ROI, 700% increase in customer acquisition. Roadmap: back-in-stock campaigns, price-drop notifications.
3. **Conversational commerce and voice shopping** — smart assistants, embedded chat, messaging apps. Case: Avis WhatsApp assistant — **70% of queries handled, 39% cost saving in one year**. Notes accessibility value for visually impaired users.
4. **Visual search and image recognition** — computer vision, image tagging, visual matching. Strongest in fashion and home décor. Shortens path to purchase, lowers cart abandonment.
5. **Smart inventory and demand forecasting** — real-time forecasting, automated replenishment, **hyper-local stock optimization**, signals include weather, holidays and local events. Reduces spoilage and dead stock.
6. **Dynamic pricing and competitive intelligence** — price by real-time demand, competition, inventory level, customer behaviour; **price elasticity modelling**; competitor site/catalogue/promotion scanning. Established in air travel and electronics; fashion and FMCG accelerating.
7. **Fraud detection and transaction security** — biometric verification, real-time transaction monitoring, predictive risk scoring against chargebacks.
8. **AI-enhanced omnichannel** — CDP consolidating mobile/desktop/in-store/post-sale; consistent messaging; orchestration across web, app, email, SMS, WhatsApp.
9. **Sustainability and waste reduction** — route optimization for fuel, demand forecasting to prevent overproduction, **right-sized packaging recommendations**, **sustainability scores in product listings**.
10. **Generative AI and creative retail** — product descriptions, marketing content, outfits and room designs, 3D/AR-VR environments, virtual fitting rooms, auto-generated ads/email/landing pages.

## 5 · Oracle — Retail AI ◐

<https://www.oracle.com/ca-en/retail/ai-retail/> · <https://www.oracle.com/retail/ai-analytics/>

The most *operationally specific* of the eight sources — it names actual optimization products rather than
themes. This is where the biggest gaps in our existing plans came from.

- **Retail AI Foundation** — analytical insights driving plan / buy / move / sell decisions; the layer the other products sit on
- **Inventory Planning Optimization Cloud Service — Demand Forecasting (IPOCS)** — single view of demand driving planning, replenishment, purchasing and allocation; more accurate forecasts lower inventory, improve safety stock, improve service
- **Lifecycle Pricing Optimization** — price across an item's lifecycle
- **Promotion, Markdown and Offer Optimization Cloud Services** — lifecycle promotion, markdown and targeted-offer recommendations tied to planned business initiatives such as time-bound campaigns
- **Assortment and Space Optimization Cloud Service**
- **Advanced clustering** (store clustering)
- **Customer decision trees**
- **Demand transference** (substitution between items)
- **Size-ratio optimization that accounts for stockouts** — i.e. censored demand, explicitly
- **Item attribute extraction from free-form descriptions**, correcting short forms and misspellings, feeding demand transference / decision trees / clustering
- Inventory optimization — predict demand and optimize across the item lifecycle, adjusting deployment as consumer behaviour changes

## Caveat on all statistics above

Every number in this file is a **vendor or vendor-adjacent claim** — six of the eight sources are
marketing pages for a product (Insider One, Oracle, Emarsys, SAP, NetSuite, Innowise). They are useful as
evidence of *what the market is buying*, and as pitch material with attribution. They are not evidence that
the same result will occur here. Any number quoted in a contest deck should be attributed to its source and
paired with our own measured baseline — which is the entire point of
[I06](I06-ai-governance-measurement.md).
